### B-569 — Acute Illness follow-up: with a positive malaria test, Send to Health Centre stops looking at every other referral reason, so a child with red MUAC or severe malnutrition signs gets Medication Distribution and Follow Up only — READY · LOW frequency · tier 3 ⚠ **clinical question: if this is not intended it is tier 1 by the diagnosis rule** · CONFIRMED (code; guard test on the seam of PR #2493) · DEPLOYED · side-finding of B-567 review round 2
- **Mechanism.** The positive-test branch never reaches the no-improvement check:
  ```elm
  -- client/src/elm/Pages/AcuteIllness/Activity/Utils.elm (expectNextStepsTaskSubsequentEncounter)
        NextStepsSendToHC ->
            if malariaDiagnosedAtCurrentEncounter then
                sendToHCByMalariaTesting ageMonths0To6 diagnosis
                    || -- Medication was perscribed, but it's out of stock, or patient is alergic.
                       sendToHCDueToMedicationNonAdministration measurements

            else
                noImprovementOnSubsequentVisit currentDate person measurements
  ```
  `noImprovementOnSubsequentVisit` is where condition not improving, fever or elevated respiratory rate, red MUAC and malnutrition complications send the patient to the health centre.
- **Impact.** A follow-up with a positive malaria test and one of those reasons, but no listed danger sign, is treated at home. With a negative test the same patient is referred. On `ihangane_1102` one follow-up had a positive test with no earlier positive, and it has a referral record; vhw has none. Not introduced by PR #2493: the task list was the same before it.
- **Open question.** Whether a malaria-positive follow-up is meant to skip the other referral reasons. No written spec found for the branch.
- **Fix shape (first guess).** Add `noImprovementOnSubsequentVisit` to the malaria branch, and re-check how it interacts with fever, which is always present when the test is offered and would refer every such patient. That interaction is why this needs the clinical answer first.
