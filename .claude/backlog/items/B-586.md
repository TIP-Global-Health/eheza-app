### B-586 — Prenatal: "high risk of preeclampsia" is diagnosed from GESTATIONAL hypertension (today's or an earlier visit's), while the spec lists only chronic hypertension — so the high-risk diagnosis and Aspirin follow any gestational hypertension from 20 weeks — READY · MED · **tier 1 (standing rule: diagnosis determination)** · ⚠ **product question — confirm the spec with the program before building** · CONFIRMED by reading on `origin/develop` b62b080eb · DEPLOYED, all sites · side-finding of B-577 (filed on request 2026-11-11: "file items for 1512 and 1529")
- **Spec.** Issue #1529 (PR #1540), "Conditions for High Risk of Pre-eclampsia": "Chronic Hypertension: EITHER Medical history subactivity 'Hypertension before pregnancy' OR as a diagnosis from Vitals". The issue's comments add nothing on hypertension (only: high supersedes moderate, preeclampsia supersedes any risk).
- **Code.** Both hypertension triggers use the full list, chronic and gestational:

                byPreviousHypertensionDiagnoses =

- **Effect.** A woman whose BP first rises after 20 weeks (gestational hypertension, by the app's own classification) also gets "high risk of preeclampsia" and the Aspirin task, which the spec reserves for hypertension before pregnancy. Clinically, gestational hypertension is on the preeclampsia spectrum rather than a pre-existing risk factor — the reason the program may or may not want it counted; the code's inclusion may be deliberate, nothing in the issue trail says so.
- **Diabetes, same question, not asserted.** The spec's diabetes line has no "chronic" word ("Diabetes Mellitus" or "a diagnosis from lab results"), so gestational diabetes counting is consistent with it; mention when asking.
