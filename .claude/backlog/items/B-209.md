### B-209 — Child Wellness dashboard: the ECD donut counts a wider period than the card above it, so the two read as breakdown and denominator — 🅿 **PARKED — LEFT AS IS (user decision, 2026-08-24: "the decision is to leave as is for now")** · CONFIRMED · tier 4 · R22, from the PR #2136/#2138/#2142 reviews
- `resolveECDStatus` admits any nurse encounter with `withinOrBeforeSelectedMonth`, so the donut covers every child seen at any point **up to and including** the selected month (the stats feed spans 6 months). `countChildrenSeenForSelectedMonth` directly above it uses `withinSelectedMonth` — **that month only**.
- Consequence: a health centre that saw 20 children in July shows a card reading 20 above a donut whose denominator is nearer 150, much of it the grey "Not Assessed" slice made of children not seen in July at all. Neither window is wrong on its own — a card called "# of Children Seen" should mean this month, and an ECD standing should not expire because a child was not seen this month — but stacked vertically they invite reading the donut as a breakdown of the card.
- ⭐ **Raised INDEPENDENTLY by three separate `/code-review medium` passes** (on `children-seen-counts-children` twice and on `spv-merge-by-person`), which is why it is filed rather than left in PR comments. The B-189 "Not Assessed" slice did not create the mismatch — it existed for the on-track/behind split — but it is what makes it visible, since the extra slice is populated largely by the out-of-month children.
- 🅿 **User decision 2026-08-24: leave as is for now.** ⚠ **This is NOT a permanent refusal** — it is a product call about what the donut is for, and it can be revisited (e.g. if users report reading the donut as a breakdown of the card). What it does forbid is re-opening it from another review pass alone: the mismatch is known, deliberate for now, and recorded here.
- Fix shapes, if it is ever taken: label the donut with its own period, or scope it to the selected month and accept that a child not seen this month has no standing to show. The first preserves the clinical meaning; the second makes the two figures comparable.
